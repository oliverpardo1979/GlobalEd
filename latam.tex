\begin{abstract}
This paper decomposes changes in average monthly labor income in Latin America
into changes in educational attainment and changes in labor income within
educational groups. We use harmonized aggregates from the World Bank's LAC
Equity Lab for 14 countries between 2016 and 2024. Unlike the companion global
paper, this paper studies all employed workers and therefore includes labor
income from both employees and self-employed workers. The analysis documents
whether recent labor-income growth came primarily from educational upgrading or
from changes in remuneration within education groups and treats the
decomposition as descriptive rather than causal.
\end{abstract}

\section{Introduction}

The educational attainment of Latin America's employed population increased
over recent decades, while the wage premium associated with secondary and
tertiary education declined in many countries. These two changes can move
average labor income in opposite directions. A larger share of highly educated
workers raises average labor income mechanically when those workers earn more,
but falling remuneration within an educational group can offset that
composition effect.

This paper measures both components for 14 Latin American countries between
2016 and 2024. It asks how much of the change in average monthly labor income
came from changes in the educational composition of employment and how much
came from changes in labor income within educational groups. It also examines
whether the relative importance of the components changed during the pandemic
and the subsequent recovery.

The paper complements the global analysis in two ways. First, LABLAC measures
labor income for all employed workers rather than wages for employees only.
Second, its quarterly observations permit a more detailed analysis of recent
changes. These advantages come with a narrower geographic scope and require
careful treatment of countries with annual observations, short series, or
documented survey breaks.

\section{Data}

\subsection{LAC Equity Lab}

The World Bank's LAC Equity Lab provides mean monthly labor income and worker
counts for three harmonized educational attainment groups: low, middle, and
high. The downloaded package covers Argentina, Bolivia, Brazil, Chile,
Colombia, Costa Rica, the Dominican Republic, Ecuador, El Salvador, Guatemala,
Mexico, Paraguay, Peru, and Uruguay.

The baseline outcome is monthly labor income in 2017 purchasing-power-parity
dollars. The worker counts correspond to the same population as the income
measure. The published aggregate can therefore be closely reconstructed as the
worker-weighted average of the three education groups. The hourly-wage series
is not used in the baseline because it covers wage workers, whereas the
published worker counts cover all employed workers.

\subsection{Annualization and comparability}

Quarterly observations will be aggregated to annual values before the main
cross-country comparison. Annualization reduces seasonal sensitivity and
allows countries with different survey frequencies to enter a common design.
Chile and Guatemala, which have shorter or lower-frequency series, will be
reported separately when the common-window requirement is not met.
